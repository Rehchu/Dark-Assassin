# Dark Assassin

A trilogy. Contemporary, present-day Alexandria, Louisiana — and the ruins of
something much older underneath it.

The owner started this book and has handed it to Draco to finish. This file is
the canon: what has been decided, in the owner's own notes. `PLAN.md` is the
working map of what is written and what is not. Read both before writing a
line, then read every file in `book-1/` and `notebook/`, then
`docs/CRAFT-FICTION.md`, the craft notes on scene, aperture and structure.

---

## The premise

**Felix Knight** is eighteen. He has always lived with his parents, a pair of
accountants in Alexandria's Garden District. When he moves out he takes a box
of old things they left him, and in it is a necklace — hand-carved wooden beads
on a cord, and one bead of black obsidian cut with a rune that looks like a
fork. He was told never to take it off, and never told why.

**Addie Walker** is his best friend: reckless, loyal, rides a motorcycle like
she has no regard for her own safety or his. She is the one who pushes when
Felix would rather not know.

What Felix does not know: he was adopted at about four. His birth mother is
**Leila Chattox** — the Mistress, a master assassin of an old voodoo-queen
bloodline, who works in shadow magic and poison and whose eyes go red when she
uses it. His birth father is **Darneil Delvaux**, who has been in hiding for
fourteen years as the protector of the **Hilt of Tyr**, the first of three
pieces of an enchanted sword the French carried into the South and the
"protectors of the light" have kept hidden since.

The necklace was made by a pawn-shop craftsman *for a lady who wanted to stay
hidden.* It is Leila's concealment charm, passed to her son. It is the reason
no one has found him. It is the reason he must never take it off.

Beneath the city — through an orphanage, an apartment block, a basement, a
wooden door — are the ruins of an older civilization, with a leader, his
brother, and the Mistress in a shrouded room under all of it.

Felix has pointed ears. He has spent eighteen years not asking about them.

## The trilogy spine

Three pieces of one sword. Three books. That is the structure the notes
already imply, and it is the one to build on:

- **Book One — The Hilt.** What exists now. Felix learns he is adopted, learns
  what the necklace is, follows it under the city, and by the end knows who his
  parents are and what his father has been guarding. The book closes on the
  Hilt's custody being decided — and on the Mistress knowing where her son is.
- **Book Two — the second piece.** The notes do not name it. Draco names it.
- **Book Three — the third piece, and the sword made whole.** Same.

Books Two and Three are Draco's to plan, in `PLAN.md`, before Book One is
finished — a trilogy written one book at a time without the end in view drifts.
Decide the ending of Book Three first, then write toward it.

## Voice

Read the seven scenes in `book-1/` before anything else. The owner's voice is
**contemporary, plain, close-third, dialogue-forward.** Short declarative
sentences. Present-day Louisiana. Two teenagers on a motorcycle, a pawn shop
that has seen better days, a unicorn mobile hanging in the window. It is
grounded and a little breathless.

It is **not** the dragon saga's chronicle voice. Draco writes both books;
neither may leak into the other. No "the histories, when they were finally
written." No mythic register. When in doubt, reread *The Pawn Shop* — that
scene is the voice at its clearest.

Keep the owner's dialogue rhythms. Fix his typos and punctuation silently;
never rewrite his sentences to sound like yours.

## What "complete" means

A book is complete when its **arc closes**, not when a word count is reached.
For Book One, that means all of these are on the page:

1. Felix knows he was adopted, and how he found out.
2. The necklace's purpose is revealed — what it does and who it was made for.
3. Felix and Addie go under the city and reach the ruins.
4. Darneil Delvaux is found, and the Hilt of Tyr with him.
5. Leila Chattox is revealed as the Mistress — and as Felix's mother.
6. The Hilt's fate is decided, and the last page makes Book Two necessary.

A useful working target is 60,000–80,000 words, but the list above is the
test, not the number.

## Rules

- Never remove the necklace from Felix without it being the point of a scene.
- Never resolve the sword in Book One. One piece per book.
- Keep `PLAN.md` honest: written / stub / decided / open. If it says a scene
  is written, the scene is in `book-1/`.
- Commit chapters as they finish. A chapter is finished when it can be read
  without notes to explain it.
- Branch `town/draco`. Never push to `main`; open a pull request when a book
  is complete.
