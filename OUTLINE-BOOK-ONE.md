# Dark Assassin — Book One: The Hilt — 24-chapter outline

A plan, not prose. Draco drafts against this once the owner approves the shape.
Target: **24 chapters**, long and atmospheric like *Homeland* (Legend of Drizzt,
Book One) — roughly **3,000–3,500 words a chapter, ~78,000–84,000 words total**.

## The feel we're borrowing from *Homeland*

*Homeland* is dark, dread-soaked, and dense, and it earns its length two ways we
can use here without betraying the owner's plain, contemporary, close-third voice:

1. **Two interwoven threads.** Salvatore cuts between Drizzt and the machinations
   of Menzoberranzan. We cut between the **overworld** (Felix and Addie, present-day
   Alexandria, investigative, grounded) and the **underworld** (the Mistress hunting
   at night, the leader and his brother in the ruins, Darneil's fourteen-year vigil).
   The underworld chapters carry the dread; the overworld chapters carry the heart.
2. **Scene depth, not purple prose.** Length comes from staying *in* a scene —
   sensory detail, real-time dread, dialogue that does work — not from mythic
   narration. The owner's voice rule holds: short declarative sentences, plain
   words, close third. Atmosphere, not filigree.

## The spine (from the owner's notes + canon)

Felix finds adoption papers → the orphanage is now apartments → an unlocked
basement → a wooden door out of place → down and down to the ruins of an older
civilization the city was built on top of → the leader of the ruins and his
brother → the Mistress, an assassin who slips to the overworld to "practice"
(the murders the police call random), her power passed down her bloodline →
Felix learns he is of that bloodline → Darneil, his father, hidden fourteen
years as protector of the **Hilt of Tyr** (first of three sword pieces) → a first
clash → the Hilt's fate decided → the Mistress knows where her son is. Book Two
is made necessary.

## Planting ledger (highlights)
- The black-haired "new" barista (Ch 3/5) → the Mistress (Ch 15) → his mother (Ch 22).
- The letters + red wax seal (Ch 3/7) → read/opened in the ruins (Ch 14–18).
- The figure across the street; the unicorn mobile that turned with no wind (Ch 7) → the Mistress was already watching (Ch 10, 15).
- Felix's pointed ears (Ch 1 on) → the bloodline (Ch 16).
- Janet Walker "knows more than she says" (Ch 2) → What Janet Knew (Ch 17).
- The murders the police call random (Ch 10) → "practice," and the overworld cost (Ch 19–21).
- Never resolve the sword: only the **Hilt** is decided in Book One; the second piece is named on the last page (Ch 24), not found.

## Rules kept
- The necklace never leaves Felix except where it is the point of a scene.
- Present-day Alexandria throughout; the ruins are *under* the real city, not a portal-world.
- The owner's plain contemporary close-third voice — atmosphere through scene, not mythic narration.
- Walker, never Griffiths.
