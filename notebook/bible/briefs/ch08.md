# Chapter 08 — Riverbend

## POV
Felix. Close third, one focal person for the whole chapter. Addie's two faces (the leasing office, the call to Janet) and her half of every phone call are seen and heard from outside; Felix does not know why she goes quiet, and the reader is only a half-step ahead of him.

## When / where
Saturday (D3), 10:00 am–2:00 pm. Overcast, warmer than Friday, dead still; the wind does not get up until dusk (Ch09).

Locations, in order:
1. Felix's apartment — second floor of an old house on a side street off Jackson, Garden District; the kitchen table with the folder on it; the window with no curtains.
2. The curb outside; Jackson Street; the river road along the levee on the east side, including **the low place** where the road dips toward a storm-drain grate (dry today).
3. **Riverbend** — the parking lot (Building C side); the leasing office in **Building A** with the brass plaque in its lobby; the landscaped strip behind **Building C**; the levee at the back of the lot; the stairwell of Building C; the door under the stair marked **MAINTENANCE**; the top of the concrete steps beyond it.

## Purpose
The lot in daylight turns the header on the adoption papers into a real place with a plaque and a stair, and two phone calls — Diane's three instructions and Janet's "don't go in the basement" — tell Felix his adoption was arranged by people who knew exactly what the necklace was, so that the unlocked door under Building C stops being a curiosity and becomes a place someone is trying to keep him out of.

## Beats
1. **10:00, the kitchen table.** Felix has had the folder open and closed since seven. The note (*do not take this off under any circumstance*), the three sealed envelopes he does not touch, the wax he does not look at. He has checked the beads are under his shirt four times since he woke. Diane's two texts. He thinks about the coffee-shop woman's mouth at his ear and does not let himself get as far as the word she used (the whisper stays off the page; the reader sees only that there is a thing he is not thinking). Addie's text from 9:40: *10. Riverbend. Bring the folder — the office might have records. Shoes you can walk in.* He does not want to bring it. He brings it, under his hoodie, because she said to.
2. **The curb.** Addie with the spare helmet held out. Felix: "Slow." Addie: "I'll drive like your mom." She rides slower than Friday, and that is worse — he can feel her being careful, which means she thinks today matters. Jackson Street to the river road; the levee on their left like a long green wall; the river smell. At **the low place** the road dips to a storm-drain grate with leaves caught in it, dry. Addie shouts back over her shoulder: "That floods." He looks at the grate going by and it is only a grate. (Plant → Ch12.)
3. **10:30, the lot.** Three brick-faced three-storey buildings, A, B, C, vinyl balconies, a flag on the leasing office, a landscaped strip, the levee at the back. Felix's travel-blogger eye, in his own plain words: it looks like every complex in every town he has ever written up; he saw these balconies in Cork. Nothing to look at. That is the first thing that gets to him — that a place with his name on a piece of paper looks like nothing. She parks Building C side. Helmets locked to the bike.
4. **10:40, the leasing office in A.** Saturday hours on the glass: 10–2 (plant → Ch09: the lot goes quiet after two). The weekend agent, **Kayla**, mid-twenties, bored and kind. The brass plaque in the lobby: the name — *Alexandria Orphanage* — and *operated by the Sisters*; Felix reads on a wall the words he has in bold under his hoodie. Addie asks about records; Kayla: nothing was kept, the building came down before the developer bought the lot, the files "went to the diocese, I think, or Catholic Charities in Baton Rouge, you'd have to write." Felix shows her only the header of the first page (Alcide said do not show the letters around; he does not). Kayla: "Oh, honey." Two things she knows, said as gossip: (a) "C's built on the old foundation — developer saved money. That's why it floods. Maintenance is always down there." (b) "There's a lady comes by every few months. Says she's thinking about a unit in C, looks at it, never takes it. Reading glasses on a string. Nice lady. Sad, kind of." Addie goes quiet. Felix sees her go quiet and cannot say why (he knows Janet; he has seen the glasses on the cord a hundred times; he does not put it together — he is reeling, and the reader is allowed to). (Plant → Ch17.)
5. **11:15, the lot.** Addie: "I'll carry that. You keep losing things." The folder goes into her backpack; it does not come out again in this act. Felix lets her, and is relieved, and is ashamed of being relieved.
6. **11:20–11:40, Felix calls Diane** from beside the bike; Addie walks ten feet off and does not pretend well. The Knights' scene, short and whole, in dialogue: Diane picks up on the first ring and cries and apologizes for crying. He asks plain questions and she answers them like a woman who has rehearsed for fourteen years and still can't do it. **The three things** the man said at the door: *keep it with him; never tell him until he leaves home; when he leaves, give him the box.* A man brought him, "in the rain — your father says it was raining like the end of the world" — through the front door, and did not stay, and did not say his name. A woman from the orphanage did the papers "and asked us not to keep her name, so we didn't. Your father thinks she goes to our church." (She does not say Walker. She never will.) Felix: "Did you know what it was? The necklace?" Diane: "We were told to keep it with you. We kept it with you. It came in that envelope. We never opened it. Your father put it in the folder so it would never get separated from your papers." Felix: "Why didn't you make me wear it?" Diane: "He said *with* you, honey. We did what he said." Then: "Are you wearing it?" — "Yes." — "Come to church tomorrow. Come to lunch. Please." He says he'll try. Robert says something orderly in the background. Felix hangs up and stands there with the phone. Addie does not ask; he tells her the three things anyway, flat, the way Diane said them.
7. **Felix's private beat, short.** The kindest people he knows kept a box for fourteen years because a stranger in the rain told them to. His whole childhood was a set of instructions somebody else wrote. He touches the beads through his shirt. He is angrier at the man in the rain than at his parents and does not know why that is.
8. **11:45, Addie calls Janet**, walking away down the strip behind C. Felix hears her half only: "Mom. The unit you looked at at Riverbend. When was that?" — "Because I'm standing in it. In the lot." — she stops walking — "In the *what*?" — "You meant *what*?" She hangs up and comes back white. "She said don't go in the basement. Just like that. Then she said she meant the flooding — that the buildings flood." Felix: "How does your mom know it has a basement?" Addie doesn't answer, which is an answer. (Plant #54 → Ch17.) Felix, for the first time, sees Addie not want to know something.
9. **12:00–1:15, the strip and the levee.** They walk the lot the way you walk a place looking for a thing you can't name. Felix finds the line in the grass behind C where the ground is different — older brick showing at the edge of the slab, a footprint bigger than Building C, the wrong shape. His history-love in one breath: *somebody built here before the orphanage, before the city got this far, and then somebody built on top of that* — his sentences, not narration. Addie photographs it. He wants to go home; she keeps him there with small true things: Kayla said maintenance is always down there; the office closes at two.
10. **1:30, Building C's stairwell.** A woman with laundry; a kid's bike against the wall; ordinary Saturday. Under the stair, the door: **MAINTENANCE**. A hasp and a padlock — the padlock hanging open on the staple. Addie: "It should be locked." She lifts it off. The door opens on concrete steps going down into a dark that smells of wet and of something burnt and sweet. Felix's phone as a flashlight. On the third step down, a **wet bootprint**, drying at the edges, too big for either of them, pointing *up*. (Plant → Ch13.) Below, the dark does the thing a person does when they have been still and decide to be stiller. A sound that is not water. Felix pulls the door to. "Not now." Addie: "There's nobody down there." Felix: "There's somebody down there." (Both true: Armand's two men.)
11. **2:00.** Across the lot the leasing office lights go off and Kayla's car leaves. The lot goes Saturday-quiet. Addie: "We wait till it's emptier and go down with the phone lights." Felix: "Or we go home." Addie: "Or we sit on the levee for an hour and *you* decide. Not me. You." He looks at the door under the stair. The chapter ends with the two of them walking up the back of the levee to sit in the gray light with the river below, the bike where it is, the door behind them unlocked, and Felix's hand at his collar checking the beads for the fifth time that day.

## Voice & tone
The owner's overworld voice at its plainest. Short declarative sentences; close third on Felix; dialogue does the work. The dread is a padlock hanging open and a bootprint pointing the wrong way, never a sentence telling us to be afraid. The Diane call is the emotional center and it is *small*: a woman crying on a Saturday morning and apologizing for it. Felix is a little rude when scared ("Or we go home."), jokes at the wrong moment, and says "I hear you" when he doesn't. The lot's ordinariness (vinyl balconies, a flag, a kid's bike) is the point; do not gothicize it. Keep the sentences running when Felix's heart does.
